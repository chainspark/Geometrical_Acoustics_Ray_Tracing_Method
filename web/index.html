Room Acoustics Ray Tracer

Image-source ray tracing with phase · C++ → WebAssembly

About this simulator

This page simulates how sound behaves inside a rectangular ("shoe-box") room. It uses geometrical acoustics: sound is modelled as rays that travel in straight lines and bounce off the walls. Each ray also carries a phase, so the simulation shows interference. Where the direct sound and a reflection arrive in step they reinforce each other, and where they arrive out of step they cancel.

The ray tracer is written in C++ and compiled to WebAssembly, so it runs entirely in your browser. Several Web Workers share the work, which lets the page trace millions of rays per second.

How it works

  1. The source emits rays in uniformly random directions.
  2. When a ray hits a wall, it reflects like light off a mirror. Its amplitude and phase are multiplied by that wall's reflection coefficient R = |R| eiφ. A reflected ray behaves as if it came from an image source mirrored behind the wall.
  3. Receivers are small spheres. Every ray that passes through one adds a complex pressure whose phase is set by the distance it has travelled.
  4. The sound pressure level is 20 log10(|Σp| / 20 µPa).

For comparison, the page also computes the exact image-source solution: the coherent sum over all image sources up to the same reflection order. With enough rays, the ray-traced result converges to it. The Difference map and the line plot show how close they are.

Using the page

  • Preset loads a ready-made scenario. Case I and Case II are the validation cases: a single reflecting floor, with the receiver moved perpendicular or parallel to it.
  • Change the room, the walls, the source or the frequency. With Re-run automatically on, the results update as you type.
  • Room view: the 3D room, with wall shading proportional to |R|, sample ray paths and the SPL slice. Drag to rotate.
  • SPL map: the level on a slice through the room, as a 2D heat map or a 3D surface.
  • Along the receiver line: ray-traced vs exact SPL. Hover to read values.
  • More rays reduce noise. A larger receiver radius also reduces noise, but blurs fine detail.

Validation

The test suite checks the ray tracer against the exact solution. Case I has an RMS error of about 0.15 dB, Case II about 0.4 dB, and a free-field (anechoic) case under 0.1 dB.

Limitations

The room must be an axis-aligned box. Reflections are specular (mirror-like), and the reflection coefficients don't depend on the angle of incidence. Scattering, diffraction and air absorption are not modelled.
